# SOMA - Crew Health Monitoring

SOMA is a web application that helps astronauts assess their own health during long space missions and gives them clear guidance on what to do next. The crew medical officer on board supervises the whole crew from the same system.

Built for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026, challenge "Create Health Monitoring Software for Astronauts on Space Missions".

## The challenge

Longer missions (Artemis, future crewed flights to Mars) expose astronauts to the five hazards of human spaceflight mapped by NASA's Human Research Program: space radiation, isolation and confinement, distance from Earth, gravity fields and hostile closed environments. These hazards can lead to immune changes, bone loss, cardiovascular events, poor nutrition and behavioral health problems.

Astronauts must be able to assess their own health during a mission. The challenge asks for a visual tool that:

- collects health indicators from the user (blood pressure, heart rate, blood sugar, body temperature, sleep hours, mood and others);
- explains how spaceflight hazards affect those indicators and when a result needs attention from the crew or from flight surgeons;
- gives guidance on prevention and treatment;
- presents metrics clearly, for example with a clickable human figure, color coding, dynamic charts, routine prompts and gamification;
- does not assume the astronaut already knows how to take each measurement.

## How SOMA answers it

| Challenge item | In SOMA |
|---|---|
| Self-assessment with health indicators | Daily self-check: resting heart rate, blood pressure, SpO2, body temperature, sleep, blood glucose, mood and comments |
| Human figure with body status | My Status shows a body map; each zone is colored by the worst related reading, with callouts to the values |
| When a result needs attention | Thresholds per indicator, automatic alerts on the server, and a "Needs your review" list for the medical officer |
| Guidance and explanations | "How to measure?" help on every field, and a Health guide with the 7 body zones, hazards, risks and actions per level |
| Dynamic charts | Trends: 7, 14, 30 or 180 days per indicator with the normal range shaded |
| Color coding | Red critical, amber caution, green normal, applied to values and status labels |
| Routine prompts and gamification | Daily checklist of assigned activities with points and progress |
| Crew and ground support | Medical officer view of the whole crew, alert handling, messages to crew members and an Excel report |

## Features

- Login with JWT and three roles: crew member, crew medical officer, administrator.
- My Status: body map, latest readings, today's checklist and latest events.
- Self-check: live evaluation while typing, draft saving, one record per day, history with calendar filter (all, week, day) and pagination.
- Trends: charts per indicator and a plain-language summary of what changed.
- Alerts: generated by server rules; the medical officer can acknowledge, resolve, reopen and undo.
- Health guide: body zones, spaceflight hazards, recommendations and references.
- Crew (medical officer): crew overview table, crew health totals with a pie chart, expandable "Needs your review" list, contact astronaut, Excel report.
- Messages: the medical officer sends routine or urgent messages; crew members see them under the bell next to their name.
- Users (administrator): create accounts, reset passwords, activate or deactivate access.
- Light and dark mode, responsive layout, English interface.

## Requirements

- Java 21
- MongoDB running on `localhost:27017`
- Maven, or the included wrapper (`mvnw` / `mvnw.cmd`)

## Run

1. Set a JWT secret of at least 32 characters (recommended):
   - Windows PowerShell: `$env:JWT_SECRET="a-long-random-secret-of-at-least-32-characters"`
   - Linux/macOS: `export JWT_SECRET="a-long-random-secret-of-at-least-32-characters"`
   - IntelliJ: Run > Edit Configurations > Environment variables.

   Without it the server generates a temporary key and sessions end when it restarts.
2. Start the application:
   - `./mvnw spring-boot:run` (Windows: `mvnw.cmd spring-boot:run`), or run `SomaApplication` from IntelliJ.
3. Open `http://localhost:8080`.

On first start, `DataSeeder` loads a demo mission, crew, 7 days of measurements, activities, alerts and a sample message. It only runs when the database is empty: to reload the demo data, drop the `soma` database in MongoDB Compass and restart.

## Usage

Crew member

1. Sign in and review My Status. The headline names the most important issue.
2. Open Self-check, enter today's readings and select Evaluate my status. Use "How to measure?" if unsure.
3. Check Trends to see how each indicator evolves, and the Health guide for actions per body zone.
4. Mark activities in today's checklist and read messages under the bell.

Crew medical officer

1. Crew shows every crew member sorted by severity (Crew overview) and the totals with a pie chart (Crew health).
2. "Needs your review" lists the crew members in critical or caution status; expand a row to see the issues, open the full record or contact the astronaut.
3. Alerts: filter by status and level, acknowledge, resolve, reopen or undo the last action.
4. Export Excel report downloads a formatted .xlsx of the crew status.
5. The medical officer also has My Status, Self-check and Trends for their own health.

Administrator

1. Users: create accounts (crew members and medical officers must be linked to an astronaut), reset passwords, activate or deactivate access.
2. Astronauts, missions and crews are managed through the API (Swagger UI) for now.

## Health indicators and thresholds

Values are educational references. Mission parameters can change the limits marked with *.

| Indicator | Caution | Critical |
|---|---|---|
| Sleep | below 6.5 h* | below 5 h |
| Resting heart rate | above 85 or below 45 bpm | 120 bpm or more, or below 40 |
| Systolic blood pressure | above 130 mmHg* | 160 mmHg or more |
| Oxygen saturation (SpO2) | below 95 %* | below 90 % |
| Body temperature | above 37.5 °C* | 38.5 °C or more, or below 35.5 °C |
| Blood glucose | below 70 or above 180 mg/dL* | below 54 or above 250 mg/dL |
| Mood (1 to 5) | 2 | 1 |
| Stress (1 to 10) | 6 or more | 8 or more |
| Accumulated radiation | 80 % of the mission limit* | mission limit reached (600 mSv in the demo) |
| Cardio exercise | below 30 min | - |

## API overview

All endpoints except login and Swagger require `Authorization: Bearer <token>`.

| Method and path | Purpose | Access |
|---|---|---|
| `POST /api/auth/login` | Sign in, returns token and user | Public |
| `GET /api/astronautas/{id}` | Astronaut profile | Staff or self |
| `POST /api/metricas` | Register a measurement (self-check) | Staff or self |
| `GET /api/metricas/astronauta/{id}/rango` | Measurements in a date range | Staff or self |
| `GET /api/alertas/astronauta/{id}` | Alerts of an astronaut | Staff or self |
| `PATCH /api/alertas/{id}/reconocer`, `/resolver`, `/reactivar` | Handle alerts | Staff |
| `GET /api/actividades/astronauta/{id}?fecha=` | Daily activities | Staff or self |
| `POST /api/mensajes` | Send a message to a crew member | Staff |
| `GET /api/mensajes/mios` | My messages | Authenticated |
| `/api/usuarios/**` | User management | Admin |

Staff means administrator or crew medical officer. Full list in Swagger UI: `http://localhost:8080/swagger-ui.html`.

## Data sources and references

- NASA Human Research Program, Risks of Human Spaceflight: Human Physiology. https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/risks/#Human-Physiology

SOMA is an academic prototype. Its thresholds and guidance do not replace the judgment of a physician or flight surgeon.

## Known limitations

- The demo data is generated by the seeder; OSD-569 is used as a reference and is not imported.
- One daily self-check per astronaut per day (server rule).
- Messages are checked every 60 seconds, not in real time.
- If Swagger UI fails to load, update `springdoc-openapi` to a 3.x version compatible with Spring Boot 4.
